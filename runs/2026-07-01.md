## 1. Hormozi item studied

**$100M Leads: hooks, "more, better, new," and the Hook-Retain-Reward framework.**
Source: web search on Hormozi's hook-writing method and the "more, better, new" scaling
multiplier (creator-economy summaries and a landing-page application piece; search
2026-07-01).

Takeaways:
1. The hook is the first five seconds of attention, whether that's an ad, a thumbnail, or
   (for an organic visitor) the literal search-result title and snippet. A weak hook there
   means the buyer never reaches the page at all, no matter how good the on-page copy is.
2. Winning hook pattern: "How to [dream outcome], even if [biggest insecurity]." The page's
   H1 already uses this ("How to launch a premium digital product in 5 days, even if you've
   never shipped anything before"), but the `<title>` and meta description predating it were
   a flat, unhooked feature description with no curiosity gap or audience call-out.
3. Call out the specific avatar by name so the right stranger self-selects. The page's own
   locked avatar language ("solopreneur paralyzed by all the steps") is a sharper hook than
   the generic "solopreneurs" the old meta description used.
4. "More, better, new": once a hook works, repeat it in more places (a scaling multiplier).
   For a single landing page, that means echoing the hero's proven H1 hook into every
   external-facing surface, search title, OG card, Twitter card, and schema description, not
   inventing a second, weaker one for each.
5. Risk reversal and price transparency raise hook quality too: naming the guarantee and
   price in the description pre-qualifies the click, raising completed-purchase rate per
   click even if raw CTR is flat.

Full notes appended to `runs/_hormozi-study-log.md`.

## 3. Concrete improvements (3)

1. **Search-result title and social-card titles now carry the page's proven hook instead of
   a flat brand tagline.** Every organic search click and every social share card was, until
   today, presenting a weaker, unhooked headline than the one already proven on the page
   itself. *Why it converts:* per Hormozi's hook framework, the headline people see before
   they ever land is the first and highest-leverage multiplier, "more, better, new" means
   repeating a working hook everywhere instead of writing a second, weaker one for the entry
   point. Front-loading "How to launch... even if..." also survives Google's title
   truncation better than the old title, which buried the hook after the brand name.
2. **Meta/OG/Twitter descriptions now call out the specific avatar and answer Time
   Delay/Effort before the click.** The old description was a feature list; the new one
   opens with "For solopreneurs paralyzed by planning," Hormozi's audience call-out
   principle, then states "one focused sitting a day" and "30-day guarantee" up front.
   *Why it converts:* pre-qualifies the click (the wrong visitor self-selects out before
   spending a click), and pre-answers the two objections (time, risk) most likely to bounce
   a cold visitor, raising completed-purchase rate per click even if raw CTR holds flat.
3. **Product schema description now matches the hook used everywhere else.** Search engines
   sometimes surface the Product schema's own description in rich results, and it previously
   read as a bland restatement disconnected from the page's actual hook. *Why it helps SEO /
   consistency:* keeps every machine-readable and human-readable entry point (title, meta,
   OG, Twitter, schema) saying the same sharpened thing, so there is no weaker fallback
   version anywhere a bot or platform might pull from.
